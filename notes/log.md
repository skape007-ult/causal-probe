### 19:40 Tier 2 code written and smoke-tested; expectations
**Honesty note:** the two smoke tests below (40 items / 8 pairs, qwen0.5b) ran before I wrote these expectations, so for qwen0.5b they are not blind. They are blind for qwen1.5b and gemma2b.
- Smoke, steering (n=40, L15, Corr2Cause): gold-probe direction flips 0% of answers at every scale up to 8 (random directions: up to 33%); diff-of-means flips 95–100% from scale 2; model-output direction flips 97–100% from scale 1.

**Expectations for the full runs.**
- Steering. What I would have said before the smoke test: gold-probe direction has a moderate causal effect, weaker than the model-output direction, stronger than random. After the smoke test I expect instead: gold-probe direction ≈ no effect (≤ random) in all three models; diff-of-means and model-output directions flip most answers at scale ≥ 2; ablating the gold-probe direction changes the logit diff by less than ablating the model-output direction. *Surprise:* the gold-probe direction flipping answers in the larger models; or accuracy improving when steering all items in the same direction (it cannot, by construction, if the direction is just "say Yes").
- Why the probe direction may be inert: logistic regression weights are discriminative, w ∝ Σ⁻¹(μ₁−μ₀); they point along low-variance directions that separate classes, which need not be directions the downstream layers read. Diff-of-means has no Σ⁻¹ and usually steers better (known from the literature on steering/"refusal direction"-style work).
- Patching. Expect: (i) minimal-pair sensitivity D small but positive on average for the larger models (they are near chance), (ii) information moves from the differing tokens to the final position in the middle third of layers, at similar *relative* depth across models, (iii) random-donor control recovery ≈ 0 at the differing tokens and at the final position early, but possibly non-zero late at the final position (any clean run carries a generic "answer" state), (iv) a handful of heads + MLPs carry most of the attribution, attribution-vs-real correlation > 0.8. *Surprise:* recovery spread evenly over layers; negative D on average (model prefers the wrong member of the pair); attribution failing validation.

### 18:25 First full-size result of the corrected adjacency test (Qwen 0.5B, final token, layer 15), against the 18:10 expectation
- *Expected:* a contrast of 5 to 25% of the full-mediation benchmark at the better site, less at the final token; erasure removing at most a quarter of the dependence.
- *Found:* translation contrast +0.110 against a benchmark of 0.112 (98%), above all 20 norm-matched controls; per template +0.040, −0.044, −0.037, −0.255, −0.183 against observed gaps of +0.050, −0.042, +0.055, −0.230, −0.113. Erasure (fresh-probe AUROC 0.876 → 0.434) changes the gap by −0.026, +0.013, +0.020, +0.247, +0.131: the dependence is gone for the collider and confounder templates. Moving across the class gap along the probe's weight direction does nothing (0.001 logits). **My expectation was wrong by a factor of four or more.**
- *Why I did not take it at face value.* At the final token, late in the network, the answer is close to a linear function of that one activation. If so, translating by the difference between two classes' mean activations must reproduce the difference between their mean answers, whatever the classes are. That is a statement about where the answer is, not about an adjacency feature. Two checks:
  1. *Geometry (CPU, from the cache).* The per-template class differences are not one direction. For Qwen 0.5B at this site the "parent" difference has cosine −0.4 to −0.6 with the other four, which agree with each other at +0.7 to +0.9, and each has cosine ±0.4 to ±0.8 with the direction along which the answer logit covaries with the activation, with the sign of that template's observed gap (one exception: descendant). The same picture at layer 9 and, weaker, at the end of the hypothesis. For Qwen 3B at layer 25 all five are positively aligned (+0.1 to +0.75), although the observed gaps are +0.90, +1.61, +0.64, +0.21 and −2.34.
  2. *Transfer (new arm, tiny run: 24 items per template).* Translating an item of template g by template h's class difference gives an effect that depends on h only: the columns of the 5 × 5 matrix are constant (for example the collider vector moves parent items by −0.277 and collider items by −0.252). Off-diagonal contrast with the source template's sign +0.112 [+0.110, +0.113]; with the target's sign −0.043.
- *Reading.* For Qwen 0.5B at the final token and layer 15 the result is a localisation: by that layer the part of the answer that depends on adjacency is linearly present at the final token, and each template's class difference is that template's answer shift. It is not evidence that the model holds an adjacency feature which the answer computation reads. The probe's weight vector being inert adds nothing either way (a read-out direction is not where the variance lives).
- *Consequence.* The transfer arm is now part of the test (commit `5c31791`). The informative sites are the earlier layer and the end of the hypothesis, where the answer is not yet formed at the steered token; and Qwen 3B, where the class differences do share a direction while the observed gaps have opposite signs. Prediction for those: if a shared adjacency encoding is read in a template-specific way, the transfer matrix will have rows, not columns, of constant sign.
