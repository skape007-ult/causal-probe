# Forty questions, with answers

Four levels of ten. Level 1: definitions and what was done. Level 2: why each design choice was made and what each control rules out. Level 3: what each result does and does not show, including the retracted and corrected claims. Level 4: what a professor would ask.

How to use it: cover the answer, say yours aloud, then compare. Each answer ends with **Evidence**, which says where to look in the repository. Where it matters, the answer separates what is **established** from what is **not established**. Every result number is filled in from `results/*.json` when this file is built. "EXPLAINER question N" refers to the numbered list in `EXPLAINER.md`, "Questions a professor would ask".

Short names used below: **LD** is the answer logit difference (the model's score for "Yes" minus its score for "No"; positive means it answers Yes). **Original pool** is the first, label-balanced Corr2Cause probing set. **Debiased pool** is the second one, built so that no single part of the prompt predicts the label.

## Level 1. Definitions and what was done

**1. What is the research question, in three parts?**
When a small language model answers a causal-reasoning question wrongly: (a) can the right answer still be read from its internal numbers, (b) does the model use that information, and (c) where in the network does the answer take shape? "Internal numbers" means activations: the vectors the network computes for each token at each layer. Part (a) is tested with probes, part (b) with steering, part (c) with the logit lens and activation patching. The report's current answers are: (a) no more than a text-only classifier can extract; (b) the experiment neither shows nor excludes a causal role; (c) patching locates where a prompt change arrives, not a judgement.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "1. Question and summary".

**2. What does one benchmark item look like, and what is the gold label?**
A Corr2Cause item has a premise and a hypothesis. The premise lists every correlation and every conditional independence among a few variables. The hypothesis is one causal claim, such as "A directly causes B." The gold label is the benchmark's correct answer: valid if the claim holds in every causal graph that fits the premise, invalid otherwise. A CLadder item is a short story with quoted probabilities and a yes/no question about an association, an intervention or a counterfactual. In the Corr2Cause test split {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_description/test/frac_valid|pct1}} of items are valid; CLadder is balanced, {{benchmark_baselines.json|cladder_full_v1.5/frac_yes|pct}} yes.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "2.1 Benchmarks"; `results/benchmark_baselines.json`, `corr2cause_description`.

**3. Which models were studied, and how is a model's answer read?**
Three small open chat models were run on the laptop: Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B. Each prompt is wrapped in the model's chat template, the fixed marker tokens the model was trained to expect around a user message. The answer is not taken from generated text. It is read from the logits, the scores the model gives to each possible next token, right after the prompt: the pooled score of Yes-like tokens minus the pooled score of No-like tokens. That is LD. A check against generated text on 500 items found disagreement on {{behaviour_qwen1.5b.json|generation/c2c_test_sub/logit_vs_gen_disagree_frac|pct1}} of items for Qwen2.5-1.5B.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "2.2 Models, hardware and the cache" (paragraph "Prompt."); table `report/tables/behaviour_generation.md`. See also EXPLAINER question 1.

**4. What is the residual stream, and what exactly was saved?**
A transformer keeps one running vector per token position. Every layer reads it and adds its own contribution. That running sum is the residual stream. For every item the project saved the residual stream at every layer, at three positions: the final prompt token (where the answer is produced), the last token of the hypothesis or question, and the last token of the premise or context. The model is run once per item; all probing then runs from the saved file on the CPU. A randomly initialised copy of each model was saved in the same way as a control.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "2.2 Models, hardware and the cache" (paragraph "Cache."); `EXPLAINER.md`, "Activation cache".

**5. What is a linear probe, and how were the probes trained and scored?**
A probe is a small classifier that predicts a label from an activation vector. Here it is logistic regression, which computes one weighted sum, so success means the label is "linearly decodable". One probe is trained per layer. Its regularisation strength C (how strongly large weights are penalised) is taken from the grid 0.01, 0.1, 1. Evaluation uses 5 folds and 3 seeds, with all items that share a premise or a story kept on the same side of every split. Premises are also split once into a selection half and a report half: the layer and C are chosen on the selection half, and every reported number is on the report half.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "2.3 Analyses" (paragraphs "Probes." and "Selection and reporting."); `EXPLAINER.md` decisions D7, D11, D17.

**6. Which Corr2Cause item sets exist, and what is each one for?**
Four. The official test split (1,162 items, natural label imbalance) measures behaviour and serves as an outside check of the probes. The original pool (2,400 items: 300 premises, each with 4 valid and 4 invalid hypotheses) was the first probing set. The debiased pool (2,400 items from 1,200 premises, in 25 blocks) is the fair probing test. The renamed test split is the test split with the variable letters A to F replaced by Z to U; it checks whether behaviour depends on the letters.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "2.1 Benchmarks" (list "Item sets used") and "3.6 A fairer test: the debiased Corr2Cause pool"; decisions D5, D9, D21.

**7. What is a text-only baseline, and which ones were used?**
A text-only baseline is a classifier that sees the prompt text, or part of it, and no model. If it predicts the gold label as well as a probe does, the probe's score does not require the model to have computed anything. The ones used: TF-IDF of the whole prompt (weighted counts of words and word pairs); a prior per hypothesis template; a prior per exact hypothesis sentence (the share of training items with that sentence that are valid); TF-IDF of the question alone; and gradient boosting on parsed features, called the hand parse. A baseline that sees only one part of the prompt is a partial-input baseline. On the debiased pool a graded series of further text models was added (question 18).
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "2.3 Analyses" (paragraph "Text-only baselines") and "3.6.1 Post-hoc reading: a ladder of baselines in two groups".

**8. What do accuracy, macro-F1 and AUROC each measure, and why report all three?**
Accuracy is the share of items answered correctly. On an imbalanced set it rewards always giving the common answer: always "No" scores {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_official_splits/test/majority/accuracy|est}} on the Corr2Cause test split. Macro-F1 averages the F1 score of the two classes, so ignoring the rare class is punished: always "No" scores {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_official_splits/test/majority/macro_f1|est}}. AUROC is the chance that a randomly chosen valid item gets a higher score than a randomly chosen invalid one. It is 0.5 for an uninformative score and does not change if the model leans towards one answer by a constant amount.
**Evidence:** `EXPLAINER.md`, "Accuracy, macro-F1 and AUROC"; table `report/tables/behaviour_zero_shot.md`. See also EXPLAINER question 2.

**9. What is steering, and what is the slope?**
Steering means adding a chosen vector to the residual stream at one layer and one token while the model runs, and then reading LD. The slope is the change in LD per unit of vector added, measured with a push in both directions so that a constant offset cancels. The unit is s, the root-mean-square distance of an activation from the layer's mean activation. Three directions were tested: the gold-label probe's weight vector; the difference between the mean activation of valid and invalid items; and the weight vector of a probe for the model's own answer. The third is a positive control: it is fitted to the very quantity that steering measures.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "4.1 Steering: what does adding a decodable direction do to the answer?" (paragraph "Design."); `EXPLAINER.md`, "Steering vector".

**10. What is activation patching, and what is a minimal pair here?**
A minimal pair is two prompts with the same number of tokens and opposite gold labels that differ as little as possible. Two kinds were built: "hyp" pairs share the premise and differ in one variable of the hypothesis; "prem" pairs share the hypothesis and have two variable letters swapped throughout the premise. Patching runs the model on one prompt (the corrupt one) and overwrites its residual stream, at one layer and one group of tokens, with the values from the other prompt (the clean one). Recovery measures how far LD moves towards the clean prompt's LD. There are {{patching_qwen0.5b.json|kinds/hyp/n_pairs|int}} pairs of each kind for Qwen2.5-0.5B and {{patching_qwen1.5b.json|kinds/hyp/n_pairs|int}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "4.2 Patching: where does a change of variable reach the answer position?" (paragraph "Design."); `results/patching_qwen0.5b.json`, `kinds/hyp/n_pairs`.

## Level 2. Why each choice was made, and what the controls rule out

**11. Why are folds grouped by premise, and why do intervals resample premises?**
Items that share a premise look alike. If some were in the training fold and others in the test fold, a probe could score by recognising the premise. Grouped folds prevent that. The same items are also not independent observations, so the bootstrap resamples whole premises (2,000 resamples). The test split has 1,162 items from only {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_description/test/n_distinct_premises|int}} premises; an interval that treated items as independent would be several times too narrow. What this does not fix: with so few clusters, bootstrap intervals cover the truth less often than their nominal 95%.
**Evidence:** decision D10; `report/report.md`, "7. Limitations" (item "Few clusters"); `tests/test_data.py`. See also EXPLAINER questions 3 and 4.

**12. What is chosen on the selection half, and what does the report half guarantee?**
Three choices are made on the selection half: the probe's layer and C, the C of the TF-IDF baseline, and which text baseline counts as the strongest. Without the split, the best of some 25 noisy layer scores would be reported on the items that picked it, which inflates it. The report half guarantees that no reported item influenced any choice. It does not mean the probe was trained on half the data: training still uses cross-validation over all premises. A probe trained on the selection half only scores {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|robustness/trained_on_selection_half_only_accuracy|ci}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B on the debiased pool, against {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/gold_probe/accuracy|ci}} for the headline probe.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "2.3 Analyses" ("Selection and reporting."); table `report/tables/debiased_robustness.md`; decisions D11, D17. See also EXPLAINER question 12.

**13. What does the shuffled-label control rule out, and why twenty shuffles?**
The probe is trained on scrambled labels. If it then scores above 0.5 on held-out items, label information is leaking through the pipeline, for example through the folds. On the debiased pool, {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/A0_single_input/probe_on_shuffled_labels_null/n|int}} shuffles give a mean of {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/A0_single_input/probe_on_shuffled_labels_null/mean|num}}, a standard deviation of {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/A0_single_input/probe_on_shuffled_labels_null/sd|num}} and a maximum of {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/A0_single_input/probe_on_shuffled_labels_null/max|num}}. One shuffle is one draw from that spread, not the spread. The third audit found that an earlier single-shuffle value for Qwen2.5-0.5B had been a lucky draw. For rare properties the shuffled value sits a little below 0.5; that is what shuffling an imbalanced label does, and it is not a fault.
**Evidence:** `results/probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json`, `baselines/A0_single_input/probe_on_shuffled_labels_null`; `notes/audit_debiased_raw.md`, finding 7.

**14. What does the random-init control rule out, and what can it not rule out?**
It is the same network with untrained weights. Whatever a probe reads there comes from the input and the architecture, not from anything the model learned. It is not at chance on the debiased pool: {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|all_items/random_init_probe/accuracy|ci}} for the 1.5B architecture. A random network still mixes the tokens of the prompt into the final position, and premise-level counts are perfectly readable from it. So this control rules out "the architecture alone explains the score". It does not rule out that the trained model merely exposes text features that a text classifier could compute as well; the text baselines test that. Only one random initialisation was run.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, table under "3.6 A fairer test" and "3.6.3"; `notes/audit_debiased_raw.md`, finding 2. See also EXPLAINER question 10.

**15. What does the control task rule out, and how do you read negative selectivity?**
In the control task every question type gets a fixed random label: every hypothesis sentence in Corr2Cause, every pair of graph and query type in CLadder. Nothing about causality predicts these labels; only memorising type-to-label does. Selectivity is gold accuracy minus control-task accuracy. On CLadder, for Qwen2.5-0.5B, the control task scores {{probes_qwen0.5b_cladder_C0.1.json|layers/control/16/accuracy|est}} and the gold probe {{confound_qwen0.5b_cladder.json|all_items/gold_probe/accuracy|est}}; selectivity is {{probes_qwen0.5b_cladder_C0.1.json|selectivity_by_layer/16|signed}}. Reading: the probe has capacity to spare and the activations identify the question type clearly, yet the answer is barely decodable. On Corr2Cause the control task was also high ({{probes_qwen1.5b_c2c_probe_C0.1.json|layers/control/21/accuracy|est}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B), a hint that the probes read which hypothesis is asked. That turned out to be the shortcut.
**Evidence:** table `report/tables/probes_summary.md`; decision D8; `notes/log.md`, last paragraph of "00:05 Independent Tier 1 audit". See also EXPLAINER question 9.

**16. Why is the end-of-premise position a built-in check, and what did it show?**
At the last token of the premise the model has not read the hypothesis. In a pool where every premise has valid and invalid hypotheses, nothing at that position can tell the label. A probe there must be at chance, and on the debiased pool it is: {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|positions/premise_end/accuracy|ci}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B. On CLadder the same check comes out differently. For Qwen2.5-0.5B the probe at the end of the context scores {{confound_qwen0.5b_cladder.json|positions/premise_end/accuracy|ci}}, and reading the question adds {{confound_qwen0.5b_cladder.json|positions/premise_end/final_minus_this_accuracy|signedci}}. So CLadder's small probe signal is largely about the story, not about the answer to the question.
**Evidence:** table `report/tables/probe_positions.md`; `report/report.md`, "3.3 Probes and controls", observation (3). See also EXPLAINER question 9b.

**17. How is the debiased pool built, and what does each rule buy?**
Rule one: every premise contributes exactly one valid and one invalid hypothesis, so a classifier that sees only the premise scores 0.50. Rule two: only the {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_description/train_hypothesis_strings/n_both|int}} hypothesis sentences that occur with both labels are used, each as often valid as invalid, so a classifier that sees only the hypothesis scores 0.50. Rule three: the pool is made of 25 blocks that each satisfy both rules, and folds are whole blocks, so the balance holds in every training set and every test set, not only in the pool as a whole. Measured: hypothesis-only {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_debiased_pool/baselines/hypothesis_only/accuracy|est}}, premise-only {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_debiased_pool/baselines/premise_only/accuracy|est}}, whole-prompt TF-IDF {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_debiased_pool/baselines/full_text_tfidf/accuracy|ci}}. The combination of premise and hypothesis still predicts the label: parsed features reach {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_debiased_pool/baselines/parsed_features/accuracy|ci}}. The pool is not pre-registered. It was designed after the first audit and revised after seeing text-baseline results, before any model was run on it.
**Evidence:** decision D21; `results/benchmark_baselines.json`, `corr2cause_debiased_pool`; `notes/audit_debiased_raw.md`, "Verified and found correct".

**18. On the debiased pool, why do the usual controls prove nothing, and what replaces them?**
Any classifier that sees one part of the prompt is at 0.50 by construction. So is any model that adds up evidence from the premise and from the hypothesis separately, such as a linear bag-of-words of the whole prompt. Beating them shows only that the pool was built correctly. Label information can come only from an interaction: what a premise feature means must depend on which hypothesis is asked. The replacement is a ladder of interaction models, ordered by how much they bind, that is, tie premise statements to the variables named in the hypothesis. Group (a) binds nothing: one model per hypothesis template on premise counts or premise words, and the random-init probe. Group (b) binds: counts of statements for each queried variable, a lookup of statements naming the pair, per-sentence n-gram models, the hand parse, and a stack of the last two. The result is the rung at which the probe sits.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "3.6.1 Post-hoc reading: a ladder of baselines in two groups"; `notes/audit_debiased_raw.md`, finding 2; `EXPLAINER.md`, "Confound controls versus reference baselines".

**19. In the steering experiment, why shuffled-label directions as controls, why two units, and why a positive control?**
A probe's weight vector points into directions where activations vary little. A difference of class means points into directions where they vary a lot. A direction drawn uniformly at random resembles neither. The fair control for each is the same construction applied to shuffled labels: 20 shuffled-label probe directions and 20 shuffled-label differences of means, next to 100 uniformly random ones. Two units are reported because one unit of s along the gold probe direction is far more than the natural spread along it: about {{steering_analysis_qwen0.5b_c2c_debiased.json|directions/gold_probe/alpha_1_in_own_sd|int}} natural standard deviations for Qwen2.5-0.5B and {{steering_analysis_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|directions/gold_probe/alpha_1_in_own_sd|int}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B on the debiased pool. The positive control shows that the method can detect an effect and how large one looks.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "4.1" ("Design." and "Matched controls"); decision D24; `EXPLAINER.md`, "Steering vector", three traps. See also EXPLAINER questions 26, 27 and 31.

**20. In the patching experiment, what do pooled recovery, the random-donor row and the attribution check each do?**
Pooled recovery is the sum of the signed effects of patching over all pairs, divided by the sum of the absolute differences in LD between clean and corrupt prompts. It was chosen because the models answer almost no pair correctly, so the usual per-pair measure on correct pairs would leave nothing. Its cost: it follows any difference between the prompts, in whichever direction. The random-donor row patches in an unrelated prompt of the same length and was meant to show that the effect is specific. It failed as a control: only {{patching_qwen0.5b.json|kinds/hyp/n_pairs_with_control|int}} of {{patching_qwen0.5b.json|kinds/hyp/n_pairs|int}} pairs (0.5B) and {{patching_qwen1.5b.json|kinds/hyp/n_pairs_with_control|int}} of {{patching_qwen1.5b.json|kinds/hyp/n_pairs|int}} (1.5B) have a donor, and the statistic is not zero when nothing specific is patched. Attribution patching estimates the effect of every attention head and MLP from one gradient. It was checked against real patching for the ten top-ranked and ten random components (correlation {{patching_qwen0.5b.json|kinds/hyp/components/pearson_attr_vs_real|num2}} for 0.5B, hyp pairs); the agreement is carried by the large components.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "4.2"; decisions D15, D16; `notes/audit_tier2_raw.md`, findings 14, 15, 18. See also EXPLAINER questions 33 and 34.

## Level 3. What each result shows and does not show

**21. Probes on the original pool score high. What does that show?**
It shows that the gold label is linearly decodable on held-out premises: {{confound_qwen0.5b_c2c_probe.json|all_items/gold_probe/accuracy|ci}}, {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_probe.json|all_items/gold_probe/accuracy|ci}} and {{confound_gemma2b_c2c_probe.json|all_items/gold_probe/accuracy|ci}} for the three models. It does not show that the models represent the answer. The hypothesis sentence alone scores {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_balanced_pool/baselines/hypothesis_only/accuracy|ci}} on that pool. Paired on the same items, probe minus hypothesis prior is {{confound_qwen0.5b_c2c_probe.json|all_items/gold_probe_minus_hyp_prior/accuracy|signedci}}, {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_probe.json|all_items/gold_probe_minus_hyp_prior/accuracy|signedci}} and {{confound_gemma2b_c2c_probe.json|all_items/gold_probe_minus_hyp_prior/accuracy|signedci}}. Against the strongest text baseline the probe is behind in every cell, for example {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_probe.json|verdict/audited/all_items_probe_minus_best_text_accuracy|signedci}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B. **Established:** the activations expose which hypothesis is being asked. **Not established:** anything beyond what a shallow reader of the prompt could compute.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "3.3" and "3.4"; tables `text_baselines.md`, `audited_verdict.md`; `figures/fig3_probes_c2c_probe.png`, `figures/fig5_confound_c2c_probe.png`. See also EXPLAINER question 5.

**22. What exactly was retracted after the first audit, and why?**
On Thursday night the pre-registered criterion came out positive for Qwen2.5-1.5B on Corr2Cause and was logged as a positive result. The criterion asked: on items the model answers wrongly, is the probe above 0.5, above TF-IDF and above the template prior? The audit gave three reasons why passing it does not support the claim. The named baselines are weak. "Above 0.5 on wrong items" is not a test against chance when {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_probe.json|verdict/audited/raw_wrong_items_frac_gold_yes|pct}} of those items are gold-Yes. And since the model's answers are nearly unrelated to the gold label, the wrong items are close to a random half, so the design reduces to probe against text baseline. The audited criterion is that comparison, against the strongest text baseline; it is not met in any cell. Be ready for one detail: in the current result files the flag for that cell under the pre-registered criterion reads {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_probe.json|verdict/represents_but_doesnt_say|str}} for the raw answer and {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_probe.json|verdict/calibrated/represents_but_doesnt_say|str}} for the median-split variant, because the TF-IDF grid was extended after the audit. Cells that pass as coded today include Qwen2.5-1.5B on CLadder ({{confound_qwen1.5b_cladder.json|verdict/represents_but_doesnt_say|str}}) and Gemma-2-2B on CLadder ({{confound_gemma2b_cladder.json|verdict/represents_but_doesnt_say|str}}). All are reported and none is relied on.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "3.4", paragraph "What was pre-registered, what passed, and what was retracted."; `notes/log.md`, "23:00 qwen1.5b Tier 1 first numbers" and "00:05 Independent Tier 1 audit"; decisions D11, D19. See also EXPLAINER questions 6, 7 and 22.

**23. On the original Corr2Cause items the models' answer scores have AUROC a little above 0.5. Is that partial competence?**
For Qwen2.5-0.5B the AUROC on the test split is {{answer_bias.json|qwen0.5b/c2c_test/auroc|ci}}, although it says Yes to {{answer_bias.json|qwen0.5b/c2c_test/yes_rate|pct}} of items. So it is biased, not empty. The signal does not survive when surface cues are removed. After the variables are renamed it is {{answer_bias.json|qwen0.5b/c2c_test_renamed/auroc|ci}}; on the debiased pool it is {{answer_bias.json|qwen0.5b/c2c_debiased/auroc|ci}}. One nuance to know: for Qwen2.5-1.5B the renamed-test AUROC is {{answer_bias.json|qwen1.5b/c2c_test_renamed/auroc|ci}}, so renaming alone does not remove its signal; the debiased pool does ({{answer_bias.json|qwen1.5b/c2c_debiased/auroc|ci}}). On CLadder the answers are at chance by AUROC too ({{answer_bias.json|qwen0.5b/cladder/auroc|ci}} for 0.5B). **Established:** on the debiased pool the pooled answers of all three models are at chance. **Not established:** why a zero-shot model's score follows the surface cue at all; the report does not explain it.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "3.1 Behaviour", table `answer_bias.md` and the paragraph "Reading."; `results/answer_bias.json`. See also EXPLAINER question 16.

**24. The first design of the debiased pool put a hypothesis-only classifier below chance. What does that show?**
In that design every hypothesis sentence was half valid over the whole pool, and folds held out premises. A hypothesis-only classifier then scored {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_string_balanced_only_pool_rejected/baselines/hypothesis_only/accuracy|ci}} accuracy, AUROC {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_string_balanced_only_pool_rejected/baselines/hypothesis_only/auroc|est}}. The cause is arithmetic. If a sentence is exactly balanced overall and happens to be mostly valid in the test fold, it is mostly invalid in the training folds, so the training prior points the wrong way. A premise-only classifier scored {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_string_balanced_only_pool_rejected/baselines/premise_only/accuracy|ci}}, because valid items came mostly from premises with many valid hypotheses. Two lessons: "balanced overall" is not "uninformative in every fold", and a score below chance is information. Anything that reads hypothesis identity, a probe included, would have been pushed below chance. No model was run on this design.
**Evidence:** `results/benchmark_baselines.json`, `corr2cause_string_balanced_only_pool_rejected`; decision D21; `notes/log.md`, "Fri 2026-11-13 10:45". See also EXPLAINER question 19.

**25. On the debiased pool the 1.5B probe scores {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|all_items/gold_probe/accuracy|num2}}. Compared with what, and what about the 0.5B model?**
Qwen2.5-1.5B: {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/gold_probe/accuracy|ci}} at layer {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|selected_layer_gold|int}}. ## Level 4. A professor would ask

**31. All your models are at chance. Is "an answer they fail to give" even a meaningful object here?**
It is a fair objection, and the report concedes the point. The design assumed a model might compute validity internally and lose it on the way to the output. For models at chance, partial computation is the more plausible state, and the debiased-pool result fits that: the 1.5B model's activations hold about a lookup's worth of the task. Under "Not supported", the report lists the claim that either model holds a correct answer it fails to give. What the study can speak to is sub-steps, such as whether premise statements have been tied to the queried variables. **Not established:** anything about a model that can do the task. The limitations say so in their first item.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "3.6.6 What this supports" and "7. Limitations" (item "Models and scale").

**32. A linear probe at three positions found nothing beyond the text. Is that evidence of absence?**
Weak evidence. A linear probe reads one weighted sum at one position. Information could be encoded non-linearly, spread over several tokens, or sit at tokens that were not saved. The report states that a negative probing result is weak evidence. The remedy is not simply a bigger probe: a probe with more capacity can compute the answer itself from the encoded text, which is exactly what the control task guards against. So the claim is narrow: at these positions, what is linearly decodable about validity is no more than the strongest text-only baseline extracts. A non-linear probe would change the conclusion only if it beat the text baselines and came with a control showing that the probe was not doing the work, such as the same probe on the random-init copy.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "7. Limitations" (item "One probe family"); decisions D7 and D8.

**33. The debiased pool is a filtered subset. Is the result still about Corr2Cause?**
It is about a designed subset, and should be described that way. The pool uses {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_debiased_pool/n_hypothesis_strings|int}} of the {{benchmark_baselines.json|corr2cause_description/train_hypothesis_strings/n|int}} hypothesis sentences, the ones that occur with both labels; sentences that are never valid cannot be balanced and are left out. Each premise is used once, with one valid and one invalid hypothesis. {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|properties/by_property/six_variables/frac_positive|pct}} of its items have six-variable premises. So it tests whether validity can be read when single-input shortcuts are absent. It does not estimate anything on the benchmark's own label distribution. **Established:** the balance holds in every fold; the third audit checked it with its own code. **Not established:** that the result carries over to the natural distribution. The audit also did not retest the pool's construction from the raw train file.
**Evidence:** decision D21; `notes/audit_debiased_raw.md`, "Verified and found correct" and "Could not check"; `report/benchmark_text_baselines.md`, "Label-balanced pools (as used for probing)".

**34. The probe ties with the pair lookup. Does that mean the model performs a lookup?**
No. Two predictors with the same accuracy need not use the same information, let alone the same computation. What the data do support is that most of the probe's signal is shared with text-derived scores. The probe's AUROC is {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|stratified/overall_probe_auroc|ci}} overall. It falls to {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|stratified/by_stratifier/hand_parse_score_quintiles/probe_auroc_within_strata|ci}} when only items with a similar hand-parse score are compared, and to {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|stratified/by_stratifier/parse_quintile_x_4gram_quintile/probe_auroc_within_strata|ci}} within 25 cells of two text scores. **Not established:** the mechanism. Missing pieces are an item-by-item comparison of probe and lookup, a stacking test against the lookup itself (stacking was run against the hand parse and the strongest text model only), and an intervention. The report's wording is "at the level of" a lookup, which is a statement about score, not mechanism.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "3.6.1" and "3.6.4"; table `debiased_strata.md`.

**35. Three audits and many post-hoc analyses. Why should I believe any number that survived?**
Sort the claims by status. Fixed in advance: the wrong-items criterion, grouped folds, split-half selection, the cluster bootstrap. Post hoc, but only able to weaken the positive claim: the stronger text baselines, the audited criterion, the debiased pool. Post hoc and pointing in the positive direction: the probe's place above the no-binding controls, the small gain over the hand parse, the adjacency probe, the answers for "directly causes". The last group deserves the most suspicion. Its intervals are unadjusted across many cells. The headline, the stacking gain and the per-template numbers were recomputed by the auditor with separate code; that guards against bugs, not against selection. The "directly causes" effect holds in both halves of the data, but the halves are not new data. **Not established:** any of the positive post-hoc statements on a model or an item set that played no part in forming them.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "Pre-registration status.", "6. Audits" and "7. Limitations" (items "Post-hoc analysis", "Multiplicity"); `notes/log.md`, the expectations written before each run.

**36. Could the steering experiment have detected a causal role if there were one?**
Only a large one. The method itself works: the positive control moves LD by {{steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|real/model_out/slope_per_s|signedci}} per unit of s for Qwen2.5-1.5B on the debiased pool, {{steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|real/model_out/versus/shuffled_probe/ratio_to_median_abs_slope_per_s|num2}} times the median of its matched controls. For the gold direction the test is blunt. With 20 controls, the gold direction can at best rank above all of them, which is one chance in 21 under no effect; an effect two or three times the control median is not excluded. One layer and one token were tested. One unit of s is about {{steering_analysis_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|directions/gold_probe/alpha_1_in_own_sd|int}} natural standard deviations along the direction, far outside what the later layers ever see. On the debiased pool the fitted direction is itself noisy: the directions fitted on two halves of the data have cosine {{steering_analysis_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|directions/gold_probe/split_half_cosine|num2}}. And the probe is stronger at the end of the hypothesis, which was not steered. That is why the conclusion is "neither shows nor excludes a causal role" and not "unused".
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "4.1", "What may and may not be concluded"; `notes/audit_debiased_raw.md`, finding 4. See also EXPLAINER questions 29 and 30.

**37. The slope along the gold direction is negative: pushing "towards valid" lowers the Yes score. Does the model read validity backwards?**
That is not established, and the report draws no conclusion from the sign. The slope is consistent across items: on the debiased pool only {{steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|real/gold_probe/frac_items_positive_slope|pct}} of items have a positive slope for Qwen2.5-1.5B. But consistency is not specific to a meaningful direction. Among the 20 shuffled-label probe directions, which encode nothing, {{steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|families/shuffled_probe/frac_sign_consistent_directions|pct}} also move nearly every item the same way, and their slopes have a median size of {{steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|families/shuffled_probe/abs_slope_per_s/median|num}} and a maximum of {{steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|families/shuffled_probe/abs_slope_per_s/max|num}}, against {{steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|real/gold_probe/slope_per_s|signedci}} for the gold direction. A large push along almost any direction shifts LD by a similar amount for every item. The one case that stands out per natural standard deviation is on the original pool, where the gold label is confounded with the hypothesis sentence (question 29).
**Evidence:** `results/steering_controls_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json`, `real/gold_probe` and `families/shuffled_probe`; `report/report.md`, "4.1", points 1 and 2.

**38. The random-init control is one initialisation. How much weight can it bear?**
Little by itself, and the argument does not rest on it. For Qwen2.5-1.5B the gap between the trained and the random-init probe is {{confound_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|all_items/gold_probe_minus_random_init_probe/accuracy|signedci}}, large against a shuffled-label spread of {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/A0_single_input/probe_on_shuffled_labels_null/sd|num}}. For Qwen2.5-0.5B the gap is {{confound_qwen0.5b_c2c_debiased.json|all_items/gold_probe_minus_random_init_probe/accuracy|signedci}}, and the audit judged that as not above the controls. The stronger no-binding control is in any case the per-template bag-of-words of the premise ({{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/A_no_binding_interaction/template_x_premise_bag_of_words/accuracy|est}}), not the random network ({{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/A_no_binding_interaction/probe_on_random_init_model/accuracy|est}}). **Not established:** how much the random-init number would move across initialisations; the report flags "one random initialisation" where it uses the figure.
**Evidence:** `report/report.md`, "3.6.1"; `notes/audit_debiased_raw.md`, findings 2 and 5.

**39. You built the "strongest text-only baseline" yourself, after seeing results. Can you not move that bar wherever you like?**
It can move in one direction only. A stronger text baseline makes "the activations hold more than the text" harder to show, never easier. That asymmetry is why adding baselines after the fact is defensible, provided it is labelled post hoc, which it is. There are two costs. First, the baselines are particular classifiers, so their scores are lower bounds on what the text gives away; the hand parse is not the ceiling, since stacking it with a per-sentence n-gram model reaches {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/B_binding_text_reference/strongest_text_only_parse_plus_4grams/accuracy|ci}}. Second, statements of the form "the probe ties rung X" depend on how well rung X was implemented; the repository's versions and the auditor's differ in detail and agree in ordering. The positive statement, "above the no-binding controls", is exposed in the other direction: a stronger control of that kind would narrow it. The claim is always relative to the baselines that were tried.
**Evidence:** decisions D18, D19 and D25 (each has a "Would change it" line); `report/benchmark_text_baselines.md`, "Limits of this note"; `notes/log.md`, "15:05 Revised debiased-pool analysis".

**40. Name one result that would change each of your conclusions.**
"No more than the text": a probe that beats the strongest text-only model on the debiased pool, with an interval that excludes zero, in a model whose answers are still at chance. Today the difference to the strongest text model is {{probe_content_qwen1.5b_c2c_debiased.json|baselines/B_binding_text_reference/strongest_text_only_parse_plus_4grams/probe_minus_this_accuracy|signedci}}. "Neither shows nor excludes use": an intervention on the feature itself, at the token where the probe is strongest, whose effect exceeds every matched control and has the template-specific sign seen in the answers; or a clean null with more controls and more sites. "Patching locates a prompt difference": pairs that differ in a variable but share the gold label. If they behave like the label-changing pairs, the reading is confirmed; if they do not, patching says something about the judgement after all. "At chance": a model whose pooled answers on the debiased pool have an AUROC clearly above 0.5. These experiments are planned; the predictions for them were written down before any of them ran.
**Evidence:** decision D27; `report/report.md`, "4.2", paragraph "Not done."; `notes/log.md`, "Fri 2026-11-13 16:35 — Scope change, and expectations written before opening the Kaggle results".
