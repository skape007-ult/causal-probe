## Findings

**F1 (critical). The positive "target-sign" transfer contrast for Qwen 3B does not show that the effect follows the item's template; it is produced by the cells in which target and source signs coincide.**
File `results/adjacency_qwen3b_c2c_debiased_final_best.json`, key `transfer.off_diagonal_contrast_with_the_TARGET_template_sign` = +0.110 [+0.097, +0.124] (table `adjacency_transfer_summary.md`, first numeric column), with the JSON note that a shared feature read in a template-specific way "predicts a positive target-sign contrast". Recomputed: +0.110 (target), +0.546 (source), exact. The contrast averages 12 off-diagonal cells among the four contrast templates (signs + + + −). In the 6 cells where the two predictions coincide (parent/ancestor/descendant among each other) the mean is +0.656; in the 6 cells where they differ the target-sign contrast is **−0.435** and the source-sign contrast +0.435; the sign of the cell follows the source in 5 of those 6 and the target in 1. The 5x5 effect matrix is rank one to 92.2% of its squared norm (singular values 3.951, 1.146, 0.074, ...): effect = (sensitivity of the target: 0.10, 0.46, 0.40, 0.67, 0.41) x (push of the source vector: +0.61, +0.30, +0.15, +0.18, −0.70). For Qwen 1.5B final_best the matrix is rank one to 99.3%, target contrast −0.093, source +0.221, source sign right in 6 of 6 discordant cells. So at both final-token best-layer sites a class-mean difference acts as an answer shift that depends on the template the vector came from, for every item it is applied to. Change: report the two contrasts on the discordant cells only (or the rank-one share); drop or re-word the JSON note; do not cite +0.110 as support for target-following.

**(f) Undecided between a chance top rank and a modest real effect of an adjacency-loaded direction; it is not a property of answer-correlated directions at this site, and it is not evidence about validity as such.**
For "real": every item, block, string and template agrees; the sign agrees in all 12 arms; part of it is traceable to the parent adjacency contrast, whose push towards Yes is the dominant column of the final-token transfer matrix of the same model. For "chance": z = 2.06, p = 0.048 to 0.058, one of 12 (24) arms, rank 5 at the final token of the same model, ranks 5 and 10 in the other models at the same token, rank 3 in the adding direction, a per-SD margin of 1.6%, and a control family whose largest member is low for its spread. The existing data cannot decide between the two.
Would 100 controls settle it? No. The first 20 of the 100 are the same shuffles, the gold direction and the items are the same, so it is a refinement of one percentile, not a replication. With z ≈ 2 a normal control family would put 4.5 of 100 controls above the gold slope (P(none) = 0.010); a flat one might put none. Either way the smallest attainable p is 1/101, and Bonferroni over 12 arms needs p < 0.0042 (rank 1 among at least 240 controls), which a 2 SD excess will not deliver.
